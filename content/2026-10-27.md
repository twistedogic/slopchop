## Introducing the Cloudflare PHP Capture the Flag challenge

Cloudflare has launched a PHP Capture the Flag challenge on HackerOne to test its web application firewall against the PHP vulnerabilities behind the recent “php2shell” exploit chain, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619. The target is a deliberately vulnerable PHP 8.5.11 application running behind Cloudflare’s default WAF protections; participants must bypass the WAF, achieve code execution, and recover a periodically reset flag. To be eligible for a bounty, researchers must sign up for HackerOne, verify the flag through the challenge UI, and file a complete private report with reproduction details. The key requirement is a materially distinct WAF-bypass mechanism, since reuse of the same bypass method is treated as a duplicate regardless of differences in the PHP payload. Testing is tightly scoped to POST requests on the challenge’s /php/ endpoints, with denial-of-service, scanning, persistence, and out-of-scope testing prohibited. Cloudflare emphasizes that the WAF is not a substitute for patching PHP, and notes that its newer detection features and mitigations are available across all plans, including Free.

## What took the Internet offline in Q3 2026

Cloudflare’s Q3 2026 review of internet disruptions, based on traffic observed across its global network, records 49 incidents\: 19 government-directed shutdowns, 14 power outages, five natural disasters, and the remainder attributed to other causes. Government shutdowns were the largest category, driven by 18 exam-related shutdowns in Iraq, which occurred on a strict schedule of roughly 03:30–04:00 UTC; Iraq’s communications minister has promised a digital exam-distribution system intended to eliminate the practice. Power failure was the second-leading cause, including repeated outages in Cuba, a three-hour interconnection failure in Honduras that cut national HTTP traffic by 63%, and conflict-related blackouts in Sevastopol and elsewhere in Ukraine that reduced traffic by as much as 98%. Natural disasters included Super Typhoon Bavi in Guam, a magnitude 7.1 earthquake in Japan, a magnitude 7.4 earthquake in Colombia, and flash floods in Nepal. Politically driven disruptions included a ten-day, essentially complete shutdown in Ethiopia’s Tigray region during renewed conflict, and fiber damage across several regions of Russia’s Far East during election day. The report also lists individual network outages in Guyana, Portugal, Haiti, Turkey, and Iran, noting that while many were brief, even short interruptions can cut people off from information, coordination, safety, and assistance.

## Crossrequest KV cache exposure in the GLM5.3 Workers AI model, and our remediation with SGLang

Cloudflare discloses a tenant-isolation flaw in the GLM-5.3 Workers AI model that caused 155 responses to contain data from other customers’ requests between August 27 and September 9, 2026. The incident stemmed from two overlapping problems\: Cloudflare configured a 1,048,576-token context window while the prefill KV cache pool held only 681,280 tokens, and an SGLang bug in disaggregated prefill/decode mode failed to abort over-length requests properly, allowing decode nodes to attend over stale cache pages still containing earlier users’ data. Affected requests were limited to prompts exceeding 681,280 tokens, and Cloudflare found no evidence of deliberate exploitation. After a researcher reported the issue through HackerOne, Cloudflare reproduced it, took the model offline within 40 minutes of declaring an incident, enlarged the KV pool, patched the rejection path, validated the fix with reproduction scripts and canary markers, and restored the model the same day. The post recommends that SGLang operators upgrade to v0.5.22 or later, measure KV pool size against context length on every node, enable deferred KV release, bound Mooncake transfer batches, and turn on checksum and canary corruption detection. Beyond the immediate fix, Cloudflare describes broader hardening across its SGLang deployments and a longer-term plan to build page-level ownership checks and apply formal verification to RDMA-based KV cache transfers.
